# Jev decision and threshold inventory (updated 2026-09-24)

Jev receives `model`, `state`, and named typed `questions` through the OpenRouter Decisions API. A `noul` answer contains its yes probability (`noul`); the observed recordings do not contain a separate confidence value. The parser derives `max(p, 1-p)` where the product expects a confidence field. Thus a high yes cutoff plus `confidence >= 0.8` is one probability gate, not two independent signals. This matches the [OpenRouter Decisions API example](https://openrouter.ai/blog/tutorials/how-to-use-jev/) and [prompt-to-questions batching example](https://openrouter.ai/labs/jev/compile). The batch builder names the relevant `state.items[key]` in each question; the observed replays parsed without a transport fault.

Threshold changes must be selected on training data and reported on untouched holdout data. Since 2026-09-24 the gap cutoffs use the precision-floor rule (`scripts/calibrate_delegated_gaps.py --selection precision_floor`), chosen before looking at holdout to favour asking or rewriting more often over never acting. Split prompts from one conversation or study participant together. Do not reuse a question's cutoff for any other question or wording. The [source follow-up](evaluation-source-followup.md) found no newly ready public broad prompt-gap labels; the user's delegated model judgments now supply an explicitly model-labeled prompt cohort. The remaining question families need direct labels of sentences, inferred values, strategies, candidates, or outputs. Prompt-level gap labels alone cannot calibrate those gates.
